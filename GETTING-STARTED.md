# Getting Started with SimpleDictation

*A friendly, no-jargon walkthrough. If you can download a file and click a few switches, you can do this.*

> SimpleDictation is a little menu-bar app that turns your voice into text in any app on your Mac: hold a key, talk, let go, and what you said appears right where your cursor is. There's nothing to learn and nothing to configure to get started. Follow the few quick steps below and you'll be dictating in about two minutes.

---

## Getting SimpleDictation Set Up

Welcome! This takes about five minutes, and you only do it once. Follow along from top to bottom and you'll be dictating in no time. If a step feels unfamiliar, don't worry, we walk through each one together.

### Step 1: Open the file you downloaded

You downloaded a file with a name like **SimpleDictation-1.3.0.dmg**. The number might be slightly different on your copy (like 1.3.1), and that's perfectly fine. A ".dmg" is just Apple's tidy little package for an app, nothing scary.

Find it in your **Downloads** folder and **double-click it** to open. A small window pops up on your screen.

### Step 2: Drag the app into Applications

Inside that window you'll see the **SimpleDictation** icon next to your **Applications** folder, usually with an arrow pointing between them.

**Click the SimpleDictation icon, drag it onto the Applications folder, and let go.** That copies the app onto your Mac for keeps. The install is done.

You can now close the little window, and drag the .dmg file to the Trash if you like. You won't need it again.

### Step 3: Open it for the first time

Open your **Applications** folder, find **SimpleDictation**, and **double-click** it.

**You'll probably see a warning that the developer "cannot be verified" or that the app "can't be opened."** Don't worry, this is completely normal and safe. It shows up because SimpleDictation is sold directly rather than through the App Store, not because anything is wrong. Here's the one-time way around it:

1. Click **Cancel** on the warning (not "Move to Trash").
2. Open **System Settings** > **Privacy & Security**, then scroll down. You'll see a line saying SimpleDictation was blocked.
3. Click **Open Anyway**, then confirm when asked. Your Mac may ask for your Touch ID or password, that's expected.

*(A quicker shortcut: in your Applications folder, right-click the SimpleDictation icon and choose **Open**, then click **Open** again on the prompt. Either way works, and you only do this once.)*

After that, here's the surprise: no big window appears. Look at the **top-right corner of your screen**, up by the clock and Wi-Fi icons. SimpleDictation lives there as a small **menu-bar icon**. That little icon is your home base for switching engines, changing your hotkey, and adjusting settings.

### Step 4: Say yes to the three permission pop-ups

For SimpleDictation to hear you and type for you, your Mac will ask permission for three things, usually one at a time. **Please say yes to all three.** The app can't work without them, and none of them let it do anything sneaky. In plain words:

1. **Microphone** so the app can hear you talk.
2. **Accessibility** so it can type your words into whatever you're using (your email, a chat box, a document) and manage your clipboard history.
3. **Speech Recognition** so it can turn your voice into text using Apple's built-in speech, which works instantly with nothing to download.

For each pop-up, click the button that allows it (**OK**, **Allow**, or **Open System Settings**). If a pop-up sends you to a settings page with a switch next to SimpleDictation, just flip that switch **on**.

**About the Accessibility one:** its wording ("would like to control this computer") sounds alarming, but all it really means is typing your dictated text into other apps and managing clipboard history. It's the permission that actually makes your text appear. It's required, and if it's off you'll see the app react when you speak but nothing will ever get typed.

Once all three are on, you're ready to dictate.

### Uh oh, I clicked "Don't Allow" by mistake

Totally fine, this happens to almost everyone, and it's a quick fix. You don't need to reinstall anything, you just turn the permission back on by hand.

For whichever one you denied, click its link below. It opens the exact right settings page on your Mac. Find **SimpleDictation** in the list and make sure its switch is **on** (blue). If you don't see SimpleDictation listed yet, click the small **plus (+)** button and add it from your Applications folder.

- **Microphone:** open <x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.preference.security?Privacy_Microphone>
- **Accessibility:** open <x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.preference.security?Privacy_Accessibility>
- **Speech Recognition:** open <x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.preference.security?Privacy_SpeechRecognition>

When you flip a switch on, your Mac may ask for your Touch ID or password, that's expected. If a link ever doesn't jump you straight there, you can always get there yourself: **Apple menu** (top-left corner) > **System Settings** > **Privacy & Security**, then pick **Microphone**, **Accessibility**, or **Speech Recognition** from the list and turn on the switch next to SimpleDictation.

After you flip a switch on, it's a good idea to **quit SimpleDictation and open it again** so the change takes hold. The simplest way to quit is to press **Cmd+Q** while the app is active, then reopen it from Applications.

### A quick check that it's working

Want to make sure everything's good? Click into any text box, like a new email or a Notes window. Hold down the **fn** key on your keyboard, say a short sentence out loud, then let go. Your words should appear right where your cursor is. If they do, you're all set.

If nothing appears, don't panic, it's almost always the **Accessibility** permission needing a nudge. Open the Accessibility link above, switch SimpleDictation **off and then back on again**, reopen the app, and try once more. That toggle fixes it the vast majority of the time.

*(Prefer a different key? You can change the hotkey to **option** anytime from the menu-bar icon, and there's also a floating mic button you can click instead of using a key.)*

### If text stops pasting later (a known quirk)

This one is worth remembering: **after you update SimpleDictation to a new version, text can suddenly stop pasting.** macOS sometimes lets the Accessibility permission go stale after an update. The fix takes ten seconds:

1. Open the **Accessibility** link above.
2. Find SimpleDictation, toggle it **off**, wait a second, then toggle it **back on**.
3. Quit and reopen SimpleDictation.

That resets it, and it's the answer the vast majority of the time. And if the app ever can't seem to hear you even with Microphone allowed, check that your Mac's input volume is turned up under **System Settings > Sound > Input**.

### Quick reference

| What you're seeing | What to check |
|---|---|
| "Cannot verify" and it won't open | Do the one-time **Open Anyway** step in Step 3. |
| Nothing happens when I hold **fn** | **Microphone** may be off. Use the Microphone link above and turn SimpleDictation on. |
| I can talk but no text appears | **Accessibility** permission. Toggle it off then back on, then reopen the app. |
| Text broke right after an update | Same fix: toggle **Accessibility** off then on, then reopen the app. |
| It can't hear me at all | With Microphone allowed, raise the input volume in **System Settings > Sound > Input**. |

Once all three permissions are on, you're past the hardest part and ready to dictate anywhere.

## Your First Dictation & Everyday Use

Let's get words on the screen right now. This takes less than a minute, and once you feel it work, you'll have it for good.

### Try it this second

1. Click somewhere you can type. A blank note, a text message box, an email, a search bar, anywhere with a blinking cursor works.
2. Press and **hold** the **fn** key (it's in the bottom-left corner of your keyboard; on some newer Macs it shows a small globe icon).
3. Keep holding, and just talk. Say a normal sentence like, "Hi there, this is my first time using dictation."
4. **Let go** of the fn key.

That's it. A moment later your words appear right where your cursor was, pasted in for you. You didn't touch a single letter.

That feeling of watching your voice turn into text? That's the whole app. Everything below just makes it handier.

> **Prefer a different key?** If holding fn is awkward, you can switch the trigger to the **Option** key (or another key) in Settings. Same rhythm, different key.

### Send it without touching the keyboard

Sometimes you want to fire off a message or submit a form the second you finish talking. You can do that hands-free too.

- **Double-tap** the fn key (tap it twice quickly, like a double-click) and it presses **Enter** for you.

So the rhythm for a quick text is: hold fn, say your message, let go, then double-tap. Sent, without ever reaching for the keyboard.

### Prefer to click instead of hold? Use the floating mic

If holding a key isn't your style, there's a little **microphone button** that sits on your screen.

- Click it once to **start** talking, click it again to **stop**. It works exactly like holding the key.
- **Double-click** it to send (that's the same as the double-tap Enter shortcut above, for people who'd rather click).
- While it's listening, the button glows and shows a ring that moves with the sound of your voice, so you always know it heard you.
- It's small and out of the way, and you can make it bigger or smaller if you like.

Quick tip: to change a setting or switch things up, **right-click** that floating mic. You can also click the little microphone icon in your **menu bar** (top-right corner of your screen). Either one opens the same settings.

### The handy "recent copies" trick

Here's a bonus that saves people all the time. SimpleDictation quietly remembers your **last 10 copied items**, so you're never stuck with only the most recent thing you copied.

This matters more than it sounds, because starting a dictation replaces whatever was last on your clipboard. If you'd copied something you still needed, it isn't lost. You can step right back to it:

1. Press and **hold** the **Command (⌘)** key.
2. Keep holding it, and **tap the V key** over and over.
3. Each tap steps back one item in your copy history. Stop when the one you want appears, then let go of Command.

So that phone number you copied a few copies ago is still there to grab. Nothing to set up; it's already running in the background, and it works in Chrome, Safari, Notes, and most apps.

### If your words suddenly stop appearing

Every so often, usually right after you install an update, macOS quietly forgets that SimpleDictation is allowed to type into other apps. You'll dictate and nothing shows up, with no error. The fix takes ten seconds:

1. Open **System Settings > Privacy & Security > Accessibility** (this link jumps you straight there: `x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.preference.security?Privacy_Accessibility`).
2. Find **SimpleDictation** in the list. Turn its switch **off, then back on**.
3. Try dictating again. That's usually all it takes.

### You're all set

That's genuinely everything for daily use: hold fn and talk, double-tap to send, click the floating mic if you'd rather, and cycle your recent copies with Command + V taps. Use it in any app, on any screen, whenever typing feels like too much.

## Engines & Troubleshooting

### Which "engine" should I pick? (Start with Apple Speech)

An engine is the part of SimpleDictation that actually listens and turns your voice into words. The app comes with several, and you can switch anytime from the menu bar icon in the top-right corner of your screen (or by right-clicking the floating mic button).

**Just starting out? Leave it on "Apple Speech."** Here's why:

- It works the moment you install the app. Nothing to download, no waiting.
- It's built into your Mac, so it's fast and accurate right out of the gate.
- It runs entirely on your Mac, so your voice never leaves your computer.

If Apple Speech is working well for you, there's no reason to change a thing. You can always try something fancier later, but on day one Apple Speech gets you dictating in seconds. No decisions required.

### The "Whisper" engines (an optional upgrade)

Once you've used the app for a bit, you might want more accuracy, especially for accents, names, or longer stretches of talking. That's what the **Whisper** engines are for. They also run entirely on your Mac (no internet needed once downloaded). The trade-off is that each one has to download first, and the bigger ones take up more space.

You do **not** need any of these to use the app. Here's the lineup in plain English:

| Engine | Download Size | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Apple Speech | Nothing | Instant, the default, start here |
| Whisper Tiny | ~40 MB | Fastest upgrade, decent for quick notes |
| Whisper Base | ~140 MB | A nice step up |
| **Whisper Small** | **~460 MB** | **The one most people should pick, best balance** |
| Whisper Medium | ~1.5 GB | Long-form dictation |
| Distil-Whisper Large v3 Turbo | ~600 MB | Highest accuracy, still a reasonable size |
| Moonshine Tiny | Bundled | Already inside the app, no download |

There are a few more advanced options in the menu too, but the ones above cover almost everyone.

**Our recommendation if you upgrade: Whisper Small.** It's the sweet spot of accuracy without a giant download.

### How downloading a model works (it's automatic)

You don't have to hunt down files or run anything:

1. Click the menu bar icon and open the engine list.
2. Engines you haven't downloaded yet look **grayed out**. That just means "not here yet."
3. Click one, and the download **starts on its own in the background.**
4. While it downloads, the app keeps using **Apple Speech** so you can keep dictating. You're never stuck waiting.
5. When the new engine is ready, you'll get a **notification** and the app switches over to it automatically.

Pick it, keep working, and it swaps itself in when it's ready.

### Troubleshooting (plain-language fixes)

Most hiccups come down to one of three Mac permissions. Tip: click the blue links below and the exact Settings page opens for you, no navigating required. Then find **SimpleDictation** in the list and flip its toggle **on**.

| What's happening | What it usually means | How to fix it |
|---|---|---|
| **Nothing pastes at all.** You talk, release the key, and no text shows up. | The **Accessibility** permission isn't turned on. This is the one that lets the app type into your other apps. | Open [Accessibility settings](x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.preference.security?Privacy_Accessibility), find **SimpleDictation**, and switch it **on**. If it's not in the list, click the **+** button and add it from your Applications folder. |
| **It can't hear me.** The app records but the text is empty or wrong. | The **Microphone** permission is off, or your Mac is listening to the wrong microphone. | First, open [Microphone settings](x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.preference.security?Privacy_Microphone) and switch **SimpleDictation** **on**. Then open **System Settings > Sound > Input**, make sure the mic you're actually speaking into is selected, and watch the input level bar move as you talk. If the bar stays flat, the problem is your mic or input choice, not SimpleDictation. |
| **Apple Speech isn't working.** Everything else is fine but Apple Speech won't transcribe. | The **Speech Recognition** permission was denied. Apple Speech needs it. | Open [Speech Recognition settings](x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.preference.security?Privacy_SpeechRecognition), switch **SimpleDictation** **on**, then quit and reopen the app. |
| **It worked before, but stopped after an app update.** | A known quirk: after an update, macOS can quietly "forget" the Accessibility permission even though it still looks turned on. | Open [Accessibility settings](x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.preference.security?Privacy_Accessibility), find **SimpleDictation**, and toggle it **off, wait a second, then back on**. If that doesn't do it, click **SimpleDictation**, press the **–** button to remove it, then click **+** to add it back from Applications. |
| **My model is still downloading.** The engine I picked isn't kicking in yet. | The download just hasn't finished. Big engines can take a while on a slower connection. | Nothing's broken. Keep dictating, the app is using **Apple Speech** in the meantime. You'll get a **notification** the moment your engine is ready, and it'll switch over on its own. |

### One more reassurance

If you ever feel stuck, the fix is almost always in that middle column: turn **SimpleDictation** back **on** under **Microphone**, **Accessibility**, or **Speech Recognition**. Those three permissions solve nearly every issue people run into, and you'll usually be back up and running in under two minutes.
